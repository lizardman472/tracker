# Hotel workout: revised A/B/C programme and research

Prepared for Saif Faraj, 27 November 2026 (New Zealand time).

**Status:** implemented as the default Hotel programme. Choose Hotel on Today or in Settings. The selection and its independent A/B/C rotation persist until switched back. Enable the long-band option only when available and the Nordic option only with a suitable secure ankle restraint. Normal Home/Partner programmes and their recovery plans keep their own prescriptions.

Existing curl, bridge, low-row and sissy-squat exercise IDs retain their history. New high rows, lateral lunges, pike presses, core movements and Y flyes have distinct IDs. An unfinished workout from the earlier release resumes the earlier order and sets; only a new workout uses this revision. The saved workout version survives backup/restore. Only checked, performed sets are saved.

The short option uses the first four movements with two sets each. Swap keeps the original slot's set count and optional status, including grounded pressing/core alternatives and split squats for lateral lunges. Existing storage-save and unfinished-work protections apply to mode and equipment preference changes.

## Decision

Use three full-body sessions on non-consecutive days, with a different emphasis on each day. Preserve repeatable presses, rows and leg work; add a small amount of shoulder pressing, vary the rowing emphasis, train the trunk directly twice, and distinguish muscle-building work from conditioning. Prioritise thighs, shoulders and upper chest within the hotel setup's limitations.

There is no established single best TRX hypertrophy programme. The exact exercise allocation and doses below are coaching choices informed by research, not a trial-validated package. They are designed for a temporary period before the flat and weights are ready, with approximately 55–65 minutes of strength work and 20–25 minutes of cardio within the confirmed 90-minute limit. Full sessions may take longer initially; time them rather than rushing necessary rest.

TRX plus floor space is the base. A long resistance band materially improves direct side-delt coverage. Nordic curls additionally require a suitable separate ankle restraint. A loaded backpack is an optional progression tool, not assumed equipment. Use only suitable secure anchors and stable supports.

## What the evidence supports

| Question | Finding | Application and limits |
|---|---|---|
| Can home/bodyweight training build muscle? | ACSM's 2026 overview supports progressive resistance training across equipment types [1]. A 42-person trial found arm and thigh growth with suspension training in older untrained adults [5]. | TRX is a useful training tool. That trial does not establish equivalence to weights in a trained younger adult. |
| How many sets? | A 67-study dose-response analysis found greater average growth with higher volume and diminishing returns, with direct/indirect set distinctions improving modelling [2]. | Use a manageable starting allocation; do not treat ten sets as a biological minimum or credit every compound fully to every muscle. |
| How many repetitions? | A 28-study network meta-analysis found no clear hypertrophy differences across the compared loads, while heavier loading favoured strength [4]. The review used failure-based interventions. | Moderate and higher reps are practical for TRX. The ranges below are convenient targets, not special growth zones. Easy high-rep work is not automatically equivalent. |
| How hard? | Exploratory meta-regressions associate training closer to failure with greater growth, but estimated RIR and heterogeneous interventions limit exact thresholds [3]. | Usually leave 1–3 technically sound reps. No compulsory failure, especially for new Nordics, sissy squats or pike presses. |
| How often? | Frequency is useful for distributing practice and volume; a clear independent hypertrophy advantage is less certain when dosage is considered [2]. | Three full-body days fit Saif's schedule. Not a claim that full body is universally superior. |
| How much rest? | The 2024 rest-interval review suggests a small average benefit from longer versus very short rests, with substantial uncertainty [7]. | Usually 2 minutes for demanding exercises and 60–90 seconds for accessories; longer if needed. Avoid copying 15–20-second circuit rests for all hypertrophy work. |
| Why different rows? | A 28-person EMG study found different recruitment patterns in low and high suspension rows [6]. | Keep low rows for a lat-oriented pull and use high rows for upper-back/rear-delt emphasis. EMG does not prove comparative muscle growth. |
| Do Nordics replace all posterior-chain work? | Nordic and hip-extension training produce different regional hamstring adaptations [8]. | Nordics are useful knee-flexion work; heavy hinge strength and hip-extension loading remain hotel limitations. |
| Is a sissy squat a proven superior quad builder? | Direct longitudinal evidence is limited. Squat/extension and reverse-Nordic studies concern different exercises [10,11]. | Retain supported sissy squats as a practical requested quad accessory, without claiming they equal machine extensions or uniquely target growth of one quad head. |
| Are lateral raises useful? | An eight-week within-person trial in 24 trained adults reported side-delt growth with dumbbell and cable lateral raises [9]. | Band raises are a practical extrapolation, not the exact tested intervention. TRX T/Y flyes are not demonstrated equivalents for side-deltoid hypertrophy. |
| Can sets be paired? | The 2025 superset review supports time savings with broadly similar chronic outcomes, alongside greater perceived effort and heterogeneous protocols [13]. | Alternate presses and rows when practical, preserving recovery. Do not force pairings that make strap changes or trunk fatigue limiting. |

Sources overlap in underlying trials and should not be counted as independent replications. This is a targeted review, not a systematic search. Publisher abstracts were inspected for several reviews and trials; full text was inspected where accessible. Some PubMed/PMC and publisher routes were unavailable. No exact individual gain or muscle-retention percentage is promised.

## Programme

Working sets exclude warm-ups. For unilateral movements, a set means the prescribed work on both sides; it counts once per side but takes two work bouts. A = 17 sets, B = 18, C = 17 when all accessories are completed. The main work is 15/14/15 sets; optional work adds 2/4/2. With no long band, B's lateral-raise slot is omitted, reducing B by two sets.

### A — split squat, chest and low row

| Order | Exercise | Working sets and reps | Role |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Supported split squat, both feet grounded | 3 × 8–20 per leg | Quads and glutes; minimal necessary hand assistance |
| 2 | TRX chest press | 3 × 8–20 | Chest, triceps and front delts |
| 3 | TRX low row, elbows travelling near the torso | 3 × 8–20 | Lat-oriented pull with biceps/upper-back involvement |
| 4 | Assisted Nordic curl with suitable ankle restraint; otherwise TRX hamstring curl | Nordic 2 × 4–8; curl 2 × 8–15 | Knee-flexion hamstrings |
| 5 | Band lateral raise | 2 × 12–25 | Side delts; without a band use 2 × 10–20 TRX T flyes for rear-shoulder work, accepting the side-delt limitation |
| 6 | Standing TRX fallout; reverse crunch if fallout setup/control is unsuitable | 2 × 8–15 fallout or 10–20 reverse crunch | Direct trunk work |
| 7 optional | TRX biceps curl | 2 × 10–20 | Extra elbow-flexor work after the priorities |

### B — hamstrings and shoulder pressing

| Order | Exercise | Working sets and reps | Role |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | TRX hamstring curl | 3 × 8–15 | Hamstrings; consistent hip position and controlled extension |
| 2 | Floor pike push-up | 2 × 6–12 | A scalable shoulder-press pattern; this is not the feet-suspended TRX abdominal pike |
| 3 | TRX low row | 3 × 8–20 | Second lat-oriented exposure |
| 4 | Supported split squat | 2 × 10–20 per leg | Second quad/glute exposure with less volume than A |
| 5 | TRX chest press or controlled floor push-up | 2 × 8–20 | Chest exposure alongside the pike press |
| 6 | Band lateral raise, if a long band is available | 2 × 12–25 | Side delts; omit this slot without a band rather than pretending another fly is equivalent |
| 7 optional | Single-leg floor glute bridge; replace with loaded B-stance RDL only if sufficient secure resistance is available | Bridge 2 × 10–20 per leg; RDL 2 × 10–20 per side | Extra hip-extension work; a lightly loaded RDL is not automatically a useful strength stimulus |
| 8 optional | TRX triceps extension if comfortable | 2 × 10–20 | Extra elbow-extensor work; pressing already supplies indirect work |

### C — lateral leg work, quads and upper back

| Order | Exercise | Working sets and reps | Role |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | TRX-assisted lateral lunge; supported split squat if the lateral pattern is unsuitable | 2 × 8–15 per side | Lateral leg/hip work, including adductor involvement; not a proven superior hypertrophy exercise |
| 2 | Push-up: floor or feet suspended in TRX, whichever permits controlled challenging pressing | 3 × 8–20 | Chest; suspension is optional rather than a performance requirement |
| 3 | TRX high row, through a comfortable elbow path | 3 × 10–20 | Upper back and rear delts; use a more upright angle if needed |
| 4 | Supported sissy squat | 3 × 8–15 | Quad-focused accessory, after the main push/pull work |
| 5 | Band lateral raise | 2 × 12–25 | Side delts; without a band use 2 × 10–15 easy-angle TRX Y flyes for scapular/shoulder work, without crediting these as equivalent side-delt sets |
| 6 | Side plank | 2 × 20–40 seconds per side | Direct lateral trunk work |
| 7 optional | TRX hamstring curl | 2 × 8–15 | Extra hamstring volume if recovery and time permit |

C uses three sissy-squat sets once accustomed because its main leg pattern is reduced to two sets. This replaces the earlier three-split-squat/two-optional-sissy arrangement; do not add it on top of the earlier programme.

The aim is coherent major-muscle coverage, not an isolation exercise for every anatomical muscle. Bands and future weights address real loading limitations more usefully than adding unstable combination movements solely for variety.

## Exercise execution and progression

### Starting dose

If these movements are unfamiliar after travel, begin with two sets for familiar main exercises and one easy set for each new Nordic, sissy squat, lateral lunge or pike press, leaving about three controlled repetitions. Keep a familiar comfortable dose when already established. Move toward the tables only as technique, soreness and next-session performance permit. Do not introduce several new demanding exercises at full dose in the same week.

Nordic: a separate appropriate secure ankle restraint and knee padding are prerequisites. Begin with 1 × 4–6 assisted reps. Lower only through a controllable range, using assistance to return. Eccentric-only practice can use 3–5 controlled lowers with a safe hand catch and assisted return. Do not use an unsecured barbell or hotel furniture as an assumed anchor.

Sissy squat: begin with 1 × 8–12 with generous support. Keep the movement controlled and progress a comfortable range. Knee-forward travel is part of the exercise; pain is not a target. Reverse Nordics are a different quad option and are not added on top.

Lateral lunge: initially use a modest step and range, with the working knee tracking with the foot and enough support to stay controlled. This is not a demand for a deep Cossack squat. Use the split-squat fallback if this new pattern is uncomfortable or difficult to standardise.

Pike push-up: keep hands and feet on the floor; raise the hips and press through the arms with a controlled comfortable head path. Adjust body position to obtain the lower rep bound without grinding. An easy-angle TRX chest press is an acceptable temporary fallback if this pressing pattern is unsuitable.

### Repeatable progression

1. Record exercise variant, strap length, floor position, depth, assistance, band identity/grip/stance and any secure external load.
2. Work within the rep range, usually leaving 1–3 technically sound reps. For new Nordics and sissy squats keep more reserve initially.
3. At the top of every prescribed set with repeatable control and intended reserve, make one small difficulty change and rebuild reps. A floor marker and consistent strap length help comparisons.
4. For supported legs, gradually reduce unnecessary hand assistance before adding secure load; do not remove needed balance support simply to make an exercise look harder.
5. For rows/presses, adjust body position gradually without sacrificing range or letting slipping/instability become the limiting factor. Use a grounded push-up if it better challenges the pressing muscles.
6. For TRX curls, progress hip control and range before single-leg or other more demanding variants. Avoid substituting back extension for hip control.
7. For planks, progress the quality and duration within the range, then adjust leverage; endless minutes are unnecessary. For fallouts, range is resistance: stop before the lower back arches.
8. Reps above the printed range are not biologically useless. However, repeatedly exceeding 25–30 easy reps is a practical reason to seek a harder setup rather than continually adding sets or slow-motion repetitions.

Controlled lowering is useful; extreme slow tempo, long burns and mandatory isometric finishers are not required. No automatic leverage/band increases should be awarded from rep counts alone. Stop or modify movements for rising joint discomfort. Reduce new-exercise volume if soreness materially disrupts the next session.

## Rest, order and time

Warm up with 3–5 minutes of easy movement and easy practice sets for the first leg, push and pull exercises. Use roughly 2 minutes before repeating demanding split squats, presses, rows and pike presses; about 2–3 minutes for Nordics; and 60–90 seconds for accessories, extending when performance requires it. A practical split-squat pattern allows 20–30 seconds between legs and 90–120 seconds after both.

Order reflects the day's emphasis and practical fatigue management; it is not a proven unique hypertrophy order. Where convenient, alternate a press and row with about 60–90 seconds between exercises, giving each movement a longer interval before repeating. Avoid leg/Nordic supersets that reduce control. Do not force pairing when strap changes waste more time than they save.

Plan approximately 55–65 minutes lifting and 20–25 minutes moderate cardio, with actual transitions measured. If strength repeatedly exceeds the cap, remove the explicitly optional arm/bridge/curl work first, then reduce a working set if necessary. Do not compress all rests into a breathless circuit to claim completion. Direct core work is before optional finishers because core previously proved easy to skip.

A rushed session can use two sets each of one leg pattern, a press and a row, then two sets of the day's hamstring/quad priority. Occasional reduced sessions are useful; routinely using them changes the weekly dose and requires reassessment.

## Conditioning and review

Follow with brisk walking or another repeatable moderate aerobic activity. Three 25-minute blocks yield 75 minutes; another three 25-minute brisk walks reach 150 moderate minutes. Do not automatically classify every minute of lifting/rest as aerobic activity [14]. Optional harder intervals can be developed later, without compromising leg recovery.

After two weeks, review session duration, soreness, progression and whether intended muscles limit sets. At four weeks, check consistent attendance and whether a harder setup or more resistance is needed. Add only 1–2 weekly sets to a stalled priority area when recovery is good; avoid increasing every muscle simultaneously. If workouts become easier because more assistance or shorter range is used, rep improvements are not comparable progress.

Maintaining muscle generally requires less work than initially building it, but reduced-dose studies retained meaningful loading [15]. This supports a pragmatic temporary plan; it does not guarantee that easy TRX work preserves heavy-barbell performance.

## Programme sources compared

- TRX's official exercise library gives useful movement examples [12]. Its circuit formats often use short timed rests and sometimes mix straps with weights. These are coaching resources, not head-to-head evidence for a best hypertrophy routine.
- The Bodyweight Fitness Recommended Routine offers a useful push/pull/leg/core framework and progressive exercise difficulty [16]. Its standard version also needs equipment for vertical pulling/dips and can be long. Its hinge progression includes Nordics, although Nordics mechanically train knee flexion rather than loaded hip hinging.
- Their common useful feature is repeated progressive basic movements. Neither requires copying every exercise or adopting a five-day split for this three-day hotel period. Advanced atomic push-ups, jump combinations, single-arm power pulls and pistol squats are optional skills, not automatic upgrades for muscle growth.
